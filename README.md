# Clinical Neuroanatomy Atlas

A local, browser-based 3D atlas of clinical neuroanatomy with synchronized MRI slices, arterial territory maps, pathway tracing, a syndrome/lesion mode, clinical topics, a glossary and a quiz. Everything is expressed in one coordinate frame, MNI152NLin2009cAsym RAS millimeters, so the 3D meshes, the T1/T2 slices and the label overlays line up exactly. Below the foramen magnum, where the MNI template ends, the slices continue into a spinal cord MRI reformatted from the open PAM50 template along the curve of the atlas's own cord.

The text is original prose. Every entry is cited to **open-access sources that anyone can read online for free** — StatPearls chapters on the NCBI Bookshelf, open-access journal articles in PubMed Central, and openly licensed textbooks — so the atlas can be read, checked and redistributed without a paywall. See [Sources and citations](#sources-and-citations).

## Running the app

```bash
npm install
npm run content      # validates and bundles content/ into public/data/content.json
npm run dev          # http://localhost:5173
```

`public/data/` (meshes, volumes, labels, manifest) is generated by the pipeline and is not committed. To regenerate it:

```bash
cd pipeline && uv sync && cd ..
uv run --project pipeline atlas-build      # download → volumes → BP3D select/register/mesh → atlas meshes → labels → manifest → QA
node scripts/check-data.ts                 # integrity check of the generated data
```

Individual steps: `atlas-download`, `atlas-volumes`, `atlas-bp3d-select`, `atlas-register`, `atlas-bp3d-meshes`, `atlas-atlas-meshes`, `atlas-venat`, `atlas-labels`, `atlas-pam50`, `atlas-manifest`, `atlas-qa`. Meshes that no source ships are constructed by `atlas-derived` (spinal cord segment blocks, phrenic nerves, lumbosacral trunk, fourth-ventricle choroid plexus; waypoints in `pipeline/config/derived_nerves.yaml`). The spinal cord MRI is built by `atlas-pam50` (needs `atlas-download --with spine`): it curve-reformats the straightened PAM50 template onto the centreline of the shipped cord surface and writes `cord_t2`, `cord_t1` and a spinal-level label volume on their own 0.75 mm grid, plus `public/data/volumes/cord_levels.json` with every C1–S5 level landed on that centreline. Z-Anatomy (needs the `blender/` venv with `bpy`): `blender/.venv/bin/python blender/export_zanatomy.py`, then `atlas-zanatomy-register`, `atlas-zanatomy-midline`, `atlas-zanatomy-meshes`, `atlas-manifest`. `pipeline/rebuild.sh` runs the full mesh rebuild; `pipeline/add_zanatomy.sh` adds the Z-Anatomy entries listed in it; `pipeline/add_brainstem_navigator.sh` ingests a manually downloaded Brainstem Navigator toolkit.

Meshing (`pipeline/atlas_pipeline/meshing.py`): label masks are converted to a signed-distance field, upsampled 2× trilinearly, smoothed, meshed at the zero level, Taubin-smoothed (20 iterations, λ 0.5 / μ −0.53), quadric-decimated to per-class budgets (`catalog.BUDGET`: cortical parcels 20k, subcortical 8k, brainstem/cerebellum 40k, envelope 60k) and welded across neighbouring parcels of one atlas; meshes above 12k triangles also get a 3k-triangle `*.lod.glb` stand-in. Colours and opacities are refreshed from `catalog.py` and the selection YAML files by `atlas-manifest`, so palette edits never need a re-mesh. Nerve tubes exported from Z-Anatomy get a minimum radius of 1 mm (cranial) or 1.5 mm (peripheral) with round caps.

Reference views: `node scripts/shots.mjs <tag>` (set `QUALITY=high` for the composer path) captures four standard views from the running dev server into `qa/shots/<tag>/`, and `pipeline/.venv/bin/python scripts/montage.py before after` tiles them.

## Using the atlas

- **Tree (left):** tri-state checkboxes on every system and subsystem row show or hide the whole group, an **All structures** master box with a **Defaults** button sits above the filter, and Alt-click on a group row solos it; filter by name.
- **Search (toolbar):** structures, pathways, syndromes; prefix `>` for syndromes only.
- **3D view:** left-drag orbits, right-drag pans, wheel zooms toward the cursor (eased), one finger orbits and two fingers zoom/pan on touch; drags keep going outside the canvas, damping is frame-rate independent, hover picking pauses while you drag, and heavy effects (ambient occlusion, anti-aliasing, shadows) are skipped while the camera moves and redrawn once it rests; click a mesh or the MRI slice to select, double-click to frame a structure; `Shift`+click selects without moving the slices. Presets `1`–`8`.
- **Rendering:** physically based materials with an anatomical palette (cortex pinkish-grey, white matter cream, vessels red and blue, nerves pale yellow, CSF and dura translucent); the **Quality** button switches on ambient occlusion, a soft key-light shadow and anti-aliasing (persisted). High-detail meshes load lazily behind small stand-ins.
- **Slices (bottom):** axial / coronal / sagittal position, T1/T2, peel mode, territory tint, label outlines, "all labels", and **cord MRI** — the PAM50 cord volume, fetched on demand and switched on by itself as soon as a slice reaches the foramen magnum or a structure below it is selected; it also extends the axial and coronal slice ranges down to the conus. On the cord slices the PAM50 **spinal levels** ride on the same overlay controls as the anatomical labels: "all labels" paints C1-S5 as a per-region ramp (cervical blues, thoracic greens, lumbar oranges, sacral reds), the level under the cursor is named in the MNI readout ("C5 · cervical segment"), the selected cord segment is outlined on the slice, and clicking a level selects that segment.
- **Content (right):** tabs for overview, anatomy, connections, function, blood supply, imaging (click a view to jump the slices), clinical, pitfalls and sources.
- **Pathways:** `#/pathway/<id>` lists the stations; click one to select it.
- **Syndrome mode:** `#/syndrome/<id>?step=n&side=l|r` dims the scene, highlights the involved structures, places the lesion marker, and steps through the deficits (◀ ▶ in the bar; click a row in the table). "Mirror" moves the lesion to the other side. `Esc` exits.
- **Topics:** `#/topic` lists the clinical topics by category; `#/topic/<id>` opens one and spotlights its meshes.
- **Quiz:** `#/quiz` (answer with `A`–`E`, arrows to move). **Glossary:** `#/glossary`.
- `?` shows all shortcuts; `Shift+S` saves a screenshot.

## Authoring content

Content lives as JSON in `content/data/<kind>/<id>.json`, validated by the zod schemas in `content/schema/` (`structure`, `cranial-nerve`, `pathway`, `syndrome`, `topic`, `glossary`, `quiz`). Entries are written with the Python helpers in `tools/author/` (`lib.py` for structures and topics, `synlib.py` for syndromes, `corlib.py`/`tractlib.py` for mesh-backed parcels and tracts) and the batch scripts `batchNN_*.py`; `node scripts/content/build.ts` validates, checks cross-links, word minimums, spelling, an 11-word-shingle overlap check against the private reference corpus, and bundles.

Rules: American spelling; every non-glossary entry carries at least one open-access citation; syndromes must state the crossing/side logic; imaging block mandatory; no figure or table references; all quiz vignettes original.

## Sources and citations

Counts today: **650 sources — 606 StatPearls chapters, 42 open-access journal articles (PubMed Central, plus one bioRxiv preprint), 1 openly licensed textbook page and 1 documentation page — carrying 2348 citations across 825 entries** (1–6 refs each; 2241 of them name a section that was read from the live article). No printed textbook is referenced anywhere in the shipped atlas. Two method papers whose journal versions are paywalled are cited through free copies instead: the VENAT venous atlas (Huck 2019) through its bioRxiv preprint, and the PAM50 spinal cord template (De Leener 2018) through the Spinal Cord Toolbox documentation page that describes it.

Citations are open access only. A citation is `{"ref": "<id>", "section": "...", "note": "..."}` and `ref` names a file in `content/bibliography/<ref>.json`; the build fails on an unknown ref, and `npm run citations:check` fails on any citation that is not in this form, on an unverified bibliography entry and on a bibliography entry nothing cites.

A bibliography entry records the title, authors, year, container, canonical free full-text URL, the accession that identifies it (`nbk` for the NCBI Bookshelf, `pmcid`/`doi`/`pmid` for articles), the licence and `verified: true`. **`verified: true` is only ever written by a tool from live source metadata** — never by hand:

| Type | What it is | Added with |
|---|---|---|
| `statpearls` | peer-reviewed StatPearls chapter on the NCBI Bookshelf (`https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK…/`) | `tools/cite/statpearls.py`, in bulk by `tools/cite/migrate.py` |
| `journal` | open-access article, stored with its PMCID and the PMC URL | `tools/cite/oa.py --pmcid PMC…` |
| `book` / `web` | openly licensed textbook or reference page (OpenStax *Anatomy and Physiology 2e*, UTHealth *Neuroscience Online*, Radiopaedia) with a specific section | `tools/cite/oa.py --web <url> …` (only written after the URL returns HTTP 200) |

The citation tooling lives in `tools/cite/`:

```bash
python3 tools/cite/catalog.py --terms-from-content   # harvest the StatPearls chapter catalog (cached in reference/)
python3 tools/cite/migrate.py                        # dry run: per-entry plan and match-quality table
python3 tools/cite/migrate.py --unmatched            # entries needing a manual mapping decision, with candidates
python3 tools/cite/migrate.py --apply                # write bibliography entries and rewrite every citations array
python3 tools/cite/statpearls.py --search "phrenic nerve"
python3 tools/cite/oa.py --pmc-search "claustrum connectivity review"
```

`migrate.py` derives search terms from each entry's name, synonyms, id and kind, queries the NCBI E-utilities (`esearch`/`esummary`, ≤3 requests/s, every response cached under the gitignored `reference/`), scores candidate chapters with a rarity-weighted title match, and adds system- and lobe-level chapters as supporting refs. Chapter section names (`"Structure and Function"`, `"Clinical Significance"`, …) are read from the live chapter, never guessed. Decisions the automatic pass cannot make are recorded by hand in `tools/cite/mapping.json`. Re-running `migrate.py --apply` is safe: entries whose citations are already refs are left untouched.

`tools/ref_*.py` and `tools/refcorpus.py` build an **optional private text corpus** under `reference/` used only by the build's 11-word-shingle plagiarism check (`scripts/content/plagiarism.ts`). Nothing from it is shipped, and the check is skipped when `reference/` is absent.

## Public and private editions

The build has two editions. The **private** edition is everything the pipeline can make and is what `npm run dev` and `npm run build` produce; it stays on this machine. The **public** edition is what may be redistributed — Apache-2.0 code, CC BY-SA 4.0 data and content — and is built into `dist-public/`.

```bash
npm run build:public        # atlas-manifest --public → content --public → vite → filter dist-public/data → check-public
npm run check-public        # re-run just the gate over an existing dist-public/
npx vite preview --outDir dist-public --port 5183
node scripts/shots-public-edition.mjs        # graceful-degradation checks + qa/shots/public-edition/
```

**What is excluded, and why.** A dataset leaves the public edition when its licence is marked `nc: true` (non-commercial) or `no_redistribution: true` in `pipeline/config/sources.yaml`. Nothing else is special-cased: change the flag and the edition changes with it.

| Dataset | Licence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Harvard-Oxford (FSL) | `FSL-NC` | non-commercial only |
| Diedrichsen cerebellar atlas | `CC-BY-NC-3.0` | non-commercial only |
| Brainstem Navigator | `BrainstemNavigator-NC-ND` | non-commercial, and clause 2 forbids passing derived files outside the organisation |
| PAM50 template | `PAM50-unlicensed` | the repository ships no licence at all, so derived files are treated as research-use-only |

That takes out **202 of 662 meshes (15.7 MB)** — 96 Harvard-Oxford parcels, 65 Brainstem Navigator nuclei, 34 Diedrichsen cerebellar lobules and the 7 cord segment blocks, which are Z-Anatomy geometry but are *cut at PAM50 level boundaries* and so are PAM50-derived themselves — plus the 3 PAM50 cord volumes (`cord_t2`, `cord_t1`, `labels_spine`) with `grids.cord` and the `volumes/labels_spine.json` LUT, 4 licences, 4 sources, and the 1.28 M voxels those atlases painted into `labels_anat` (zeroed, so the label volume no longer carries them either). What is left is **460 meshes (30.3 MB)**, all 16 systems, the MNI T1/T2 volumes, the remaining label volumes, and the complete authored content — 391 structures, every syndrome, pathway, topic and quiz.

**Where the outputs go.** The private `public/data/manifest.json`, `content.json` and `search-index.json` are never overwritten by a public build:

| Command | Writes |
|---|---|
| `atlas-manifest --public` (or `ATLAS_EDITION=public`) | `public/data/manifest.public.json`, `public/data/manifest.public.exclusions.json` (every excluded mesh, volume, licence and source with its reason) |
| `node scripts/content/build.ts --public` | `public/data/content.public.json`, `public/data/search-index.public.json` |
| `npm run build:public` | `dist-public/`, where those files are renamed to the ordinary `data/manifest.json`, `data/content.json`, `data/search-index.json` |

The content build validates the authored JSON against the **private** manifest either way, so an entry is never wrong just because the public edition drops its mesh; only the bundle is filtered, and `content/` itself is never edited. A structure whose meshes are all gone keeps its full text with `meshIds: []`. An `MniRef` that pointed at a dropped mesh keeps its MNI coordinate (a location, not atlas data) and loses the mesh id, so "where to look" still moves the slices.

**In the app.** A public manifest simply has fewer meshes, so the tree omits them and the cord MRI toggle hides itself when `grids.cord` is absent. Opening a structure whose mesh is gone — `#/structure/gyrus-precentral`, `#/structure/locus-coeruleus` — shows its content with a "No 3D mesh in this edition" notice instead of doing nothing.

**The gate.** `scripts/check-public.ts` walks the built `dist-public/` and fails on an excluded mesh id anywhere, a restricted licence or source id in the data, a file the public manifest does not reference, a mesh or volume file that should have been dropped, or the strings `Harvard-Oxford`, `Diedrichsen`, `Brainstem Navigator`, `PAM50` in the manifest or volume metadata. It reports, rather than fails on, two things it cannot honestly call leaks: ten ids that name both an excluded mesh and the authored entry describing it (`filum-terminale`, the cord segment blocks — the entry keeps its own id, and a structural pass proves no mesh field points at them), and those dataset names where they appear in authored prose as citations and teaching notes ("the group probability map of the Brainstem Navigator…"). Naming a dataset is attribution, not redistribution. The app bundle is scanned for mesh ids only, because `src/ui/sourceLine.ts` and the cord toggle name licences and datasets in code.

`tests/public-edition.test.ts` re-implements the exclusion rule in TypeScript, runs it over the real private manifest, and requires `atlas-manifest --public` to have dropped exactly the same set — so a change on either side shows up as a test failure — then checks that the private manifest, the private bundle and `content/` are untouched. `node scripts/check-data.ts --all` checks both manifests (`--manifest <file>` picks one, including `dist-public/data/manifest.json`).

## Not covered

- The thalamostriate vein has no mesh: along the caudothalamic groove the VENAT venous atlas never rises above a partial volume of about 0.4 and its ridge is not separable from the shoulder of the internal cerebral vein, so it stays a content-only structure. The internal cerebral, basal and great cerebral veins and the straight sinus **do** have meshes now, cut from that atlas by `atlas-venat`.
- The phrenic nerve, the choroid plexus of the fourth ventricle, the four spinal cord segment blocks and the lumbosacral trunk have no mesh in any source atlas either, so they are **derived**: constructed by `pipeline/atlas_pipeline/derived.py` (`atlas-derived`) from geometry that does exist, tagged `derived` in the manifest with the construction method, and flagged as schematic in their content entries. Z-Anatomy models one spinal root and ganglion pair per intervertebral foramen from C2 to L2 only, so the lumbosacral root meshes hold the L1 and L2 pairs and the rest of the cauda equina is a single mesh.
- The Z-Anatomy affine (`pipeline/config/zanatomy_to_mni.json`) is fitted on 31 brain landmarks (mean 3.6 mm), all within about 12 cm of the diencephalon, so nothing in the fit constrains its x-from-z shear and the mapped midline used to drift off MNI x = 0 below the skull base: −1.2 mm at C7, −3.6 mm at T12, −4.2 mm at the conus, −12 mm at the ankle. `atlas-zanatomy-midline` measures that midline (the Z-Anatomy source is mirror-symmetric about x = 0 to within 0.25 mm, over 1356 left/right object pairs plus the vertebral column, cord and sacrum), fits the drift plane and writes a `post_correction` block that `atlas-zanatomy-meshes` and `atlas-derived` apply to every vertex after the affine. Its weight is a smoothstep in Z-Anatomy source height that is exactly zero at and above the foramen magnum, so the x correction leaves every cranial vertex bit-identical, and the residual sub-cranial midline offset is under 0.35 mm along the whole cord. **Anteroposteriorly the same fit leaves the Z-Anatomy brainstem off the MNI one along its whole length**, and because that starts in the midbrain rather than below the skull base it needs its own ramp. The Z-Anatomy brainstem surfaces are dumped for the measurement by `blender/dump_brainstem_ap.py`, and two references are needed because neither covers the whole band. Below the pontomesencephalic junction the measurement is the mid-sagittal silhouette centre per 2 mm slab against the MNI aseg brainstem (label 16): the two match at that junction and the Z-Anatomy side then falls behind by −4 mm at mid-pons, −8 mm at the pontomedullary junction, −15 mm at the obex, −20 mm at the foramen magnum and −23 mm at the cervicomedullary junction, where the cord used to emerge behind the occiput. Above that junction the silhouette is **not comparable**: the aseg `Brain-Stem` label stops at the front of the cerebral peduncles (at MNI z −10 its mid-sagittal anterior boundary is y = −23, the peduncular block in front of it being labelled Ventral-DC, and Harvard–Oxford's brainstem label cuts at the same place), while the Z-Anatomy `Midbrain` mesh is a complete midbrain reaching y = −6 there, so comparing silhouette centres reports the midbrain as 9–10 mm anterior when corresponding structures put it at 2–5 mm. Through the midbrain the fit therefore uses five corresponding-structure centroids instead — inferior colliculus −5.1 mm, red nucleus −4.8 mm, superior colliculus −2.2 mm, posterior commissure −0.1 mm and pineal gland +0.9 mm, the first four from MASSP and the last the registration's own reference point — which are true correspondences and are what the affine itself is fitted on. `atlas-zanatomy-midline` fits one `ap` ramp into the same `post_correction` block from both: a Fritsch–Carlson monotone cubic through nine anatomical knots in Z-Anatomy source height, with a single interior extremum and monotone either side of it. It is exactly 0 at and above the **mesencephalic–diencephalic junction** (source z 1.6184 m, the rostral end of the `Aqueduct of midbrain`, which is above the top of the `Midbrain` object at 1.6175 m, so the whole midbrain is inside the corrected band and the diencephalon and forebrain stay bit-identical), reaches −5.2 mm at the intercollicular knot, passes through 0 at the pontomesencephalic junction and is constant +23.3 mm at and below the cervicomedullary junction (so the cord's own shape and its ≈21° slope, which already match the MNI brainstem axis, are preserved and the whole sub-cranial body inherits one rigid translation). The fit residual is 1.0 mm rms over 27 slabs and 0.43 mm over the five midbrain correspondences; `atlas-qa` gates the residual at 0.97 mm at the cervicomedullary junction (limit 1.5 mm), the per-level residual at seven named levels from the upper midbrain to the cervicomedullary junction (worst 1.33 mm, limit 2 mm), the upper anchor's height, and displacement at or above it at exactly 0 mm. The Z-Anatomy midbrain, pons and medulla centroids move from +5.5 / −4.9 / −11.9 mm off the aseg brainstem centreline to +0.4 / −0.9 / +2.3 mm. All 156 exported Z-Anatomy objects now move (126 only below the pontomesencephalic junction, 11 only in the midbrain, 19 in both bands): everything below the cervicomedullary junction by the full 23.3 mm, and in the midbrain band CN III and IV, the cerebral peduncle crura, the oculomotor nucleus and the cavernous, intercavernous, straight and inferior sagittal sinuses by up to 5.2 mm, per-mesh maxima in `report.json`. The CN III–XII roots keep their attachment to their Z-Anatomy parent to within 0.2 mm while their distance to the MNI aseg brainstem falls from 3.2 to 1.0 mm (III), 4.4 to 1.6 mm (IX) and 11.3 to 1.4 mm (XI); the corrected cord runs continuously out of the MNI aseg brainstem (`pipeline/work/zanatomy/midline/ap_*.png` and `midbrain_*.png`, `qa/shots/midline/ap-*.png`, `qa/shots/midbrain/`). Content `imagingHint`/`lesionMarker` coordinates written on a straight MNI cord axis (y ≈ −48) therefore land on the mesh down to about z = −120 mm; below that the mesh follows the body's own kyphotic curve and they still miss it.
- **The BodyParts3D vertebral arteries** were two copies of one thing and in the wrong place, and are fixed in `pipeline/atlas_pipeline/bp3d.py`. BodyParts3D lists 43 element files under `partof` for each of FMA4066 (left) and FMA3958 (right), 29 of them shared, and every one of those files is a piece of the whole vertebrobasilar tree; unioning the two element maps therefore gave both arteries — and the basilar, FMA50542 — byte-identical copies of the entire tree. The `isa` map lists exactly one element file per concept, which is that vessel alone, so those three entries now carry `tree: isa` in `bp3d_selection.yaml` (the basilar names its element outright, because `isa` lists a duplicate second copy of it). Separately, the BodyParts3D affine is fitted on brain landmarks and leaves the neck pitched too far back, so the arteries ran 15–20 mm behind the cord: measured two independent ways — the BodyParts3D brainstem against the MNI aseg brainstem, and the artery against the corrected Z-Anatomy vertebral artery, dumped for the measurement by `blender/dump_vertebral_ap.py` — the offset is 0 at the vertebrobasilar junction, +17.5 mm from just below the foramen magnum down to C2/C3 and back to 0 by C5/C6. `atlas-register --post-correction` fits a `post_correction` ramp into `bp3d_to_mni.json` (the same monotone cubic as the Z-Anatomy one, in BodyParts3D source height), pinned to exactly 0 at the vertebrobasilar junction so the basilar — a cranial mesh — does not move and the arteries stay welded to it; the residual falls from 17.6 mm to 1.95 mm, and `atlas-qa` gates that, the 1 mm cranial-displacement rule and the junction's distance to the Mouches MRA atlas. It is applied per entry (`postCorrection: true`), **not** to every sub-cranial BodyParts3D vertex: the BodyParts3D internal carotid is drawn about 18 mm anterior of its own cervical spine, so its model error already cancels the registration error and the same ramp would push it out of the carotid sheath. Figures: `pipeline/work/bp3d/vertebral_*.png`, `qa/shots/vertebral/`.
- Brainstem Navigator nuclei **are** included in this local build but never in a shared one. The toolkit needs a manual NITRC download (click-through licence), and clause 2 of its terms forbids distributing the files, or anything derived from them, outside your organisation: the 65 meshes are therefore tagged `nc` + `noRedistribution` in the manifest (licence `BrainstemNavigator-NC-ND`, verbatim terms in `public/data/licenses/`), shown with an **NC** badge in the tree, and **must be excluded from any build that is published or shared**. To rebuild them: put `BrainstemNavigatorv1.0.zip` in `pipeline/raw/manual/` (or unpack it into `pipeline/raw/brainstem_navigator/`) and run `pipeline/add_brainstem_navigator.sh` (`atlas-brainstem-nav --inventory` first). The abbreviation-to-mesh table is `pipeline/config/brainstem_navigator.yaml`: all 47 abbreviations of the two MNI label trees are mapped, 12 that MASSP20/CIT168 already provide (SN, RN, STh, VTA, PAG, SC, IC, DR, MnR, PTg, LG, MG) are skipped as duplicates, and their 7 T subdivisions (SN1/SN2, RN1/RN2, STh1/STh2 and the reticular-formation parts) are built hidden and attached to the parent structure entry.
- General neuron and glial cell biology is covered only where it touches a topic (transmitters, nerve injury, cortical layers).

Verbatim licence texts ship with the data in `public/data/licenses/<id>.txt`: the name, URL and attribution header, plus the full legal code where the licence has one under `pipeline/config/license_texts/` (`atlas-download` writes them). `public/data/LICENSE` itself is written by `atlas-manifest` from `pipeline/config/data_LICENSE.txt`, so the generated folder always carries its own licence.

**In the app:** the toolbar's **About** button and the `#/about` route open *About and credits* — the two licences with links, the derivation statement and the edition of this build (`manifest.edition`, private while non-redistributable data is in it), a table of every source in the manifest (dataset name, citation, licence linked to its text, download link, mesh count, and an "excluded from the public edition" badge on the restricted ones), and every licence text as an expandable section. Every structure, pathway and topic panel carries a **Source** line naming the datasets its meshes come from with their licences, tagging derived meshes, and linking to that panel.

# Building the data

How `public/data/` is generated, step by step, and the registration and meshing notes behind it. Everything
here is the pipeline in `pipeline/`; the app itself is described in the [README](../README.md).

A default run of the pipeline builds the **public** edition. The four restricted datasets (Harvard-Oxford,
the Diedrichsen cerebellar atlas, PAM50 and the Brainstem Navigator) are `group: restricted` in
`pipeline/config/sources.yaml`, and `atlas-download` fetches them only on the `private` branch or with
`ATLAS_ALLOW_RESTRICTED=1`. See [The two editions](editions.md).

## Regenerating `public/data/`

`public/data/` (meshes, volumes, labels, manifest) is generated by the pipeline and is not committed. To regenerate it:

```bash
cd pipeline && uv sync && cd ..
uv run --project pipeline atlas-build      # download → volumes → BP3D select/register/mesh → atlas meshes → labels → manifest → QA
node scripts/check-data.ts                 # integrity check of the generated data
```

Individual steps: `atlas-download`, `atlas-volumes`, `atlas-bp3d-select`, `atlas-register`, `atlas-bp3d-meshes`, `atlas-atlas-meshes`, `atlas-venat`, `atlas-lc-metamask`, `atlas-aan`, `atlas-labels`, `atlas-pam50`, `atlas-spine-generic`, `atlas-fudan-spine`, `atlas-cord-public`, `atlas-manifest`, `atlas-qa`. Meshes that no source ships are constructed by `atlas-derived` (spinal cord segment blocks, phrenic nerves, lumbosacral trunk, fourth-ventricle choroid plexus; waypoints in `pipeline/config/derived_nerves.yaml`), together with the public edition's vertebral-landmark cord blocks and its landmark-anchored brainstem nuclei (`pipeline/config/brainstem_landmarks.yaml`). The public edition's cortex needs `atlas-download --with public-parcellations` (CerebrA); `atlas-atlas-meshes` prints `[cerebra] skipped` and carries on without it. The spinal cord MRI is built by `atlas-pam50` (needs `atlas-download --with restricted`): it curve-reformats the straightened PAM50 template onto the centreline of the shipped cord surface and writes `cord_t2`, `cord_t1` and a spinal-level label volume on their own 0.75 mm grid, plus `public/data/volumes/cord_levels.json` with every C1–S5 level landed on that centreline. The public edition's cord MRI is built in two stages. `atlas-spine-generic` (needs `atlas-download --with spine-open`, about 160 MB) and `atlas-fudan-spine` (needs `atlas-download --with spine-open` too) each write a *straightened template* into `pipeline/work/cord_public/` — the cord resampled on a straight (arc, u, v) lattice with the discs, rootlet levels and conus tip they measured on it, in the contract documented at the top of `pipeline/atlas_pipeline/cord_public.py`. `atlas-cord-public` then lays every template it finds there on the same centreline through the same reformat, level-matching each one to the Z-Anatomy vertebral column disc by disc, blending them where they overlap, and writes `cord_t2_public`, `labels_spine_public` and `cord_public.json` / `cord_levels_public.json`. Either template alone is enough, so a build without the Fudan raw data simply ships the cervical and upper thoracic cord. Z-Anatomy (needs the `blender/` venv with `bpy`): `blender/.venv/bin/python blender/export_zanatomy.py`, then `atlas-zanatomy-register`, `atlas-zanatomy-midline`, `atlas-zanatomy-meshes`, `atlas-manifest`. `pipeline/rebuild.sh` runs the full mesh rebuild; `pipeline/add_zanatomy.sh` adds the Z-Anatomy entries listed in it; `pipeline/add_brainstem_navigator.sh` ingests a manually downloaded Brainstem Navigator toolkit.

Meshing (`pipeline/atlas_pipeline/meshing.py`): label masks (cortical parcels first closed with a 1-voxel ball — see the note below) are converted to a signed-distance field, upsampled 2× trilinearly, smoothed, meshed at the zero level, Taubin-smoothed (20 iterations, λ 0.5 / μ −0.53), quadric-decimated to per-class budgets (`catalog.BUDGET`: cortical parcels 20k, subcortical 8k, brainstem/cerebellum 40k, envelope 60k) and welded across neighbouring parcels of one atlas; meshes above 12k triangles also get a 3k-triangle `*.lod.glb` stand-in. Colours and opacities are refreshed from `catalog.py` and the selection YAML files by `atlas-manifest`, so palette edits never need a re-mesh. Nerve tubes exported from Z-Anatomy get a minimum radius of 1 mm (cranial) or 1.5 mm (peripheral) with round caps.

Reference views: `node scripts/shots.mjs <tag>` (set `QUALITY=high` for the composer path) captures four standard views from the running dev server into `qa/shots/<tag>/`, and `pipeline/.venv/bin/python scripts/montage.py before after` tiles them.

## Pipeline notes

- The thalamostriate vein has no mesh: along the caudothalamic groove the VENAT venous atlas never rises above a partial volume of about 0.4 and its ridge is not separable from the shoulder of the internal cerebral vein, so it stays a content-only structure. The internal cerebral, basal and great cerebral veins and the straight sinus **do** have meshes now, cut from that atlas by `atlas-venat`.
- The phrenic nerve, the choroid plexus of the fourth ventricle, the four spinal cord segment blocks and the lumbosacral trunk have no mesh in any source atlas either, so they are **derived**: constructed by `pipeline/atlas_pipeline/derived.py` (`atlas-derived`) from geometry that does exist, tagged `derived` in the manifest with the construction method, and flagged as schematic in their content entries. The cord blocks are cut twice. The private edition gets the **measured** cut, on planes normal to the local tangent of the cord's own centreline at the PAM50 spinal-level boundaries — which makes those blocks PAM50-derived, so the public edition may not have them. The public edition gets `spinal-segment-{cervical,thoracic,lumbar,sacral}-vert` instead: the same Z-Anatomy surface cut by horizontal planes at its own vertebral-body landmarks with the classical cord-segment/vertebra rule (`cord_segments_by_vertebrae()`, tagged `derived: "vertebral-landmark cut (Z-Anatomy geometry only)"`, `edition: "public"`, appended to the same four entries' `meshIds`). The public sacral block stops at the conus rather than running on to the caudal end of the surface: the conus tip is put at the middle of the Z-Anatomy `Intervertebral disc L1-L2` (`conus_level()`, read from `work/zanatomy/objects.json`), the classical adult level, and the remainder below that plane ships as `filum-terminale-vert` — so the public edition has a filum of its own, cut by a vertebral landmark instead of by a PAM50 spinal level. The two cuts do not agree: the vertebral filum is about half the length of the PAM50-cut one, which both entries' pitfalls say. Z-Anatomy models one spinal root and ganglion pair per intervertebral foramen from C2 to L2 only, so the lumbosacral root meshes hold the L1 and L2 pairs and the rest of the cauda equina is a single mesh.
- The Z-Anatomy affine (`pipeline/config/zanatomy_to_mni.json`) is fitted on 31 brain landmarks (mean 3.6 mm), all within about 12 cm of the diencephalon, so nothing in the fit constrains its x-from-z shear and the mapped midline used to drift off MNI x = 0 below the skull base: −1.2 mm at C7, −3.6 mm at T12, −4.2 mm at the conus, −12 mm at the ankle. `atlas-zanatomy-midline` measures that midline (the Z-Anatomy source is mirror-symmetric about x = 0 to within 0.25 mm, over 1356 left/right object pairs plus the vertebral column, cord and sacrum), fits the drift plane and writes a `post_correction` block that `atlas-zanatomy-meshes` and `atlas-derived` apply to every vertex after the affine. Its weight is a smoothstep in Z-Anatomy source height that is exactly zero at and above the foramen magnum, so the x correction leaves every cranial vertex bit-identical, and the residual sub-cranial midline offset is under 0.35 mm along the whole cord. **Anteroposteriorly the same fit leaves the Z-Anatomy brainstem off the MNI one along its whole length**, and because that starts in the midbrain rather than below the skull base it needs its own ramp. The Z-Anatomy brainstem surfaces are dumped for the measurement by `blender/dump_brainstem_ap.py`, and two references are needed because neither covers the whole band. Below the pontomesencephalic junction the measurement is the mid-sagittal silhouette centre per 2 mm slab against the MNI aseg brainstem (label 16): the two match at that junction and the Z-Anatomy side then falls behind by −4 mm at mid-pons, −8 mm at the pontomedullary junction, −15 mm at the obex, −20 mm at the foramen magnum and −23 mm at the cervicomedullary junction, where the cord used to emerge behind the occiput. Above that junction the silhouette is **not comparable**: the aseg `Brain-Stem` label stops at the front of the cerebral peduncles (at MNI z −10 its mid-sagittal anterior boundary is y = −23, the peduncular block in front of it being labelled Ventral-DC, and Harvard–Oxford's brainstem label cuts at the same place), while the Z-Anatomy `Midbrain` mesh is a complete midbrain reaching y = −6 there, so comparing silhouette centres reports the midbrain as 9–10 mm anterior when corresponding structures put it at 2–5 mm. Through the midbrain the fit therefore uses five corresponding-structure centroids instead — inferior colliculus −5.1 mm, red nucleus −4.8 mm, superior colliculus −2.2 mm, posterior commissure −0.1 mm and pineal gland +0.9 mm, the first four from MASSP and the last the registration's own reference point — which are true correspondences and are what the affine itself is fitted on. `atlas-zanatomy-midline` fits one `ap` ramp into the same `post_correction` block from both: a Fritsch–Carlson monotone cubic through nine anatomical knots in Z-Anatomy source height, with a single interior extremum and monotone either side of it. It is exactly 0 at and above the **mesencephalic–diencephalic junction** (source z 1.6184 m, the rostral end of the `Aqueduct of midbrain`, which is above the top of the `Midbrain` object at 1.6175 m, so the whole midbrain is inside the corrected band and the diencephalon and forebrain stay bit-identical), reaches −5.2 mm at the intercollicular knot, passes through 0 at the pontomesencephalic junction and is constant +23.3 mm at and below the cervicomedullary junction (so the cord's own shape and its ≈21° slope, which already match the MNI brainstem axis, are preserved and the whole sub-cranial body inherits one rigid translation). The fit residual is 1.0 mm rms over 27 slabs and 0.43 mm over the five midbrain correspondences; `atlas-qa` gates the residual at 0.97 mm at the cervicomedullary junction (limit 1.5 mm), the per-level residual at seven named levels from the upper midbrain to the cervicomedullary junction (worst 1.33 mm, limit 2 mm), the upper anchor's height, and displacement at or above it at exactly 0 mm. The Z-Anatomy midbrain, pons and medulla centroids move from +5.5 / −4.9 / −11.9 mm off the aseg brainstem centreline to +0.4 / −0.9 / +2.3 mm. All 156 exported Z-Anatomy objects now move (126 only below the pontomesencephalic junction, 11 only in the midbrain, 19 in both bands): everything below the cervicomedullary junction by the full 23.3 mm, and in the midbrain band CN III and IV, the cerebral peduncle crura, the oculomotor nucleus and the cavernous, intercavernous, straight and inferior sagittal sinuses by up to 5.2 mm, per-mesh maxima in `report.json`. The CN III–XII roots keep their attachment to their Z-Anatomy parent to within 0.2 mm while their distance to the MNI aseg brainstem falls from 3.2 to 1.0 mm (III), 4.4 to 1.6 mm (IX) and 11.3 to 1.4 mm (XI); the corrected cord runs continuously out of the MNI aseg brainstem (`pipeline/work/zanatomy/midline/ap_*.png` and `midbrain_*.png`, `qa/shots/midline/ap-*.png`, `qa/shots/midbrain/`). Content `imagingHint`/`lesionMarker` coordinates written on a straight MNI cord axis (y ≈ −48) therefore land on the mesh down to about z = −120 mm; below that the mesh follows the body's own kyphotic curve and they still miss it.
- **The BodyParts3D vertebral arteries** were two copies of one thing and in the wrong place, and are fixed in `pipeline/atlas_pipeline/bp3d.py`. BodyParts3D lists 43 element files under `partof` for each of FMA4066 (left) and FMA3958 (right), 29 of them shared, and every one of those files is a piece of the whole vertebrobasilar tree; unioning the two element maps therefore gave both arteries — and the basilar, FMA50542 — byte-identical copies of the entire tree. The `isa` map lists exactly one element file per concept, which is that vessel alone, so those three entries now carry `tree: isa` in `bp3d_selection.yaml` (the basilar names its element outright, because `isa` lists a duplicate second copy of it). Separately, the BodyParts3D affine is fitted on brain landmarks and leaves the neck pitched too far back, so the arteries ran 15–20 mm behind the cord: measured two independent ways — the BodyParts3D brainstem against the MNI aseg brainstem, and the artery against the corrected Z-Anatomy vertebral artery, dumped for the measurement by `blender/dump_vertebral_ap.py` — the offset is 0 at the vertebrobasilar junction, +17.5 mm from just below the foramen magnum down to C2/C3 and back to 0 by C5/C6. `atlas-register --post-correction` fits a `post_correction` ramp into `bp3d_to_mni.json` (the same monotone cubic as the Z-Anatomy one, in BodyParts3D source height), pinned to exactly 0 at the vertebrobasilar junction so the basilar — a cranial mesh — does not move and the arteries stay welded to it; the residual falls from 17.6 mm to 1.95 mm, and `atlas-qa` gates that, the 1 mm cranial-displacement rule and the junction's distance to the Mouches MRA atlas. It is applied per entry (`postCorrection: true`), **not** to every sub-cranial BodyParts3D vertex: the BodyParts3D internal carotid is drawn about 18 mm anterior of its own cervical spine, so its model error already cancels the registration error and the same ramp would push it out of the carotid sheath. Figures: `pipeline/work/bp3d/vertebral_*.png`, `qa/shots/vertebral/`.
- **Seven BodyParts3D duplicates of MNI-native structures are no longer shipped** — the whole brain, the two cerebral hemispheres, the brainstem, the cerebellum and the two lateral ventricles (`export: false` in `pipeline/config/bp3d_selection.yaml`; `atlas-bp3d-meshes` retires their glb, stand-in, record and `meshes.json` row). They were hidden reference layers, but they are a second specimen under a 12-dof affine fitted on deep-nucleus centroids, and measured against the shipped MNI meshes on 2026-09-09 they were off by far more than a reference layer can be: the hemispheres a mean 15–18 mm *inside* the MNI cortex over the frontal, central and parietal convexity (p95 42 mm — the BodyParts3D brain is 130 mm wide against the template's 145), the brainstem 14–25 mm posterior at the medulla, so it never met the cord (the aseg brainstem overlaps the Z-Anatomy cervical cord by 4 mm, 1.9 mm off axis), the cerebellum a mean 5 mm / p95 15 mm, the lateral ventricles a mean 10 mm / p95 33 mm. A neurologist read them as a second brainstem and a mis-registered FreeSurfer surface. The concept meshes still exist in `work/bp3d/`, because `atlas-register` fits the frame on exactly those. **Not changed:** the same affine carries every other BodyParts3D mesh, so the cortical branches of the MCA sit a median 21 mm inside the brain surface over the convexity (z > 15, |x| > 45); that needs a registration that sees the cortical surface as well as the deep landmarks.
- **Cortical parcels are closed before meshing** (`AtlasSpec.fill_radius`, set from `catalog.CORTICAL_FILL_RADIUS = 1` on CerebrA and Harvard-Oxford; `atlas_meshes.fill_parcel`). A parcel of a 1 mm group-average label volume is a ribbon two voxels thick, and sulcal CSF punches through it: the superior parietal lobule had 54 tunnels and 16 surface shells, the lateral occipital cortex 21 and 10, and a neurologist read the meshes as "eroded, maybe a reconstruction error". The reconstruction was not at fault — the shipped 20,000-triangle mesh lay a mean 0.24 mm (max 0.7 mm) from the raw marching-cubes surface of its label and held 100% of the label's volume, and neither a 60,000 budget nor less smoothing moved either number, so the decimation cap, the first suspect, is innocent. Each parcel is now closed with a 1-voxel ball, keeping only added voxels that lie inside the brain mask and in no parcel of the same atlas, so borders between neighbours do not move and no voxel already in a parcel does either. Over the 62 CerebrA parcels that removes 27% of the tunnels (1066 → 781) for +3.6% volume and takes the pits out of the renders; radius 2 would remove 52% for +9.5% and starts filling real sulci. The composite label volume is untouched, so a filled voxel is drawn by the mesh but not painted on the slice.

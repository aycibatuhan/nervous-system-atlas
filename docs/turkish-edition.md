# The Turkish edition

The Turkish interface, the clinical-prose overlays and the terminology table that decides which FIPAT term
every entry carries. Back to the [README](../README.md).

The interface exists in English and Turkish. The **TR / EN** button in the toolbar (shortcut `L`) switches the language; the choice is kept in the URL hash (`#/structure/brainstem?lang=tr`, so a link opens in the language it was copied in) and in `localStorage`, and a first visit follows the browser language. Every interface string lives in `src/i18n/en.ts` and `src/i18n/tr.ts` (289 keys; the Turkish table is typed against the English one, so a missing translation fails `npm run typecheck`, and `tests/i18n.test.ts` checks that the placeholders agree). In Turkish mode, structures, cranial nerves and pathways are named the way Turkish medical teaching names them, by their Latin term (`names.tr`, falling back to `latin`), with the English name as a secondary line in the panels, the tree and the search results; Turkish synonyms from Wikidata and Turkish Wikipedia are searchable but never displayed. Clinical prose (structure text, syndromes, topics, glossary, quiz) is translated too (see below); an entry whose translation is missing or stale carries a small *English* tag in Turkish mode; entries without a FIPAT concept (arterial territories, gaze pathways, the envelope) keep their English name in both languages. The About panel's disclaimer and credits are in both languages. `node scripts/shots-tr.mjs` captures the Turkish interface from the dev server into `qa/shots/tr-ui/`.

**Clinical prose.** Translations live as overlays in `content/i18n/tr/<kind>/<id>.json`: `{ "id", "kind", "lang": "tr", "source": <hash of the English strings>, "fields": { "<flattened path>": "<Turkish>" } }`, one string per translatable path of the English entry (`deficits[2].sign`, `anatomy.subdivisions[0].note` …). Identifiers, enumerations, citations, mesh ids and MNI numbers are never in an overlay, nor is the `name` of a structure, nerve or pathway (Turkish mode shows the Latin term); syndrome and topic names and glossary terms are. `python3 tools/i18n/prose.py extract` writes task files with the English strings and the style guide `tools/i18n/STYLE-tr.md` for the translators, `check` verifies every overlay against its entry (missing or unknown paths, a changed English source, lost cross-links, numbers that differ), and `status` prints the coverage. The content build applies the overlays and writes `public/data/content.tr.json` next to the English bundle (`content.tr.private.json` for the private edition); the app fetches it the first time Turkish is selected, and a translated entry loses its *English* tag.

The names come from a terminology table that fixes, once, which FIPAT term every atlas entry corresponds to and which Turkish names already exist for it:

```bash
pipeline/.venv/bin/python tools/i18n/terms.py fetch     # FIPAT TA2 + TNA PDFs, Wikidata, Turkish Wikipedia -> reference/terms/ (cached)
python3 tools/i18n/terms.py table                       # content/i18n/review/terms-review.csv + terms-review.md
python3 tools/i18n/terms.py show substantia-nigra       # every candidate for one entry and why it was ranked there
python3 tools/i18n/terms.py apply [--dry-run]           # write the reviewed terms into content/data/ (log: content/i18n/review/apply-log.md)
```

`fetch` parses the terminology tables of FIPAT's *Terminologia Anatomica 2* (2019, 7112 terms in five parts) and *Terminologia Neuroanatomica* (2017, 4398 terms in three chapters) from the PDFs on the Dalhousie library CDN, keeping the Latin term, Latin synonym, UK and US English, English synonyms, the "Other" column, the indentation depth and the bold heading rows; the parser was checked against the Open Anatomy TA2 viewer's copy of TA2 (all 7112 term numbers, 5 Latin and 3 English differences, every one a typo in one copy or the other). It then pulls every Wikidata item that carries a TA98 or TA2 id (5923 items: labels and aliases in English, Latin and Turkish, the TA98 Latin term, FMA/UBERON/NeuroNames ids, Turkish and English Wikipedia sitelinks) and the redirects of the 620 Turkish Wikipedia articles among them. `table` matches the 416 structures, cranial nerves and pathways to those sources by Latin term first, then English term, English synonym, and a numeral-safe fuzzy fallback, joins the sources to each other (TA2 to Wikidata by TA2 number, TA2 to TNA by Latin term) so a hit in one source nominates the linked concept in the others, prefers candidates several sources agree on and whose ancestors mention the entry's parent, and writes one row per entry with the best TA2, TNA and Wikidata concept, the Turkish label and article, a `relation` column (exact, synonym, narrower when the FIPAT term is one component of a pathway or an "A and B" entry, broader when it encompasses a subdivision such as "X, anterior division", fuzzy, related, none), and `match` / `alt` / `note` columns saying how it got there, including when one of the two FIPAT terms is a subdivision of the other. The `decision` column is the reviewer's and survives regeneration. Today 322 entries get a FIPAT Latin term and 104 a Turkish name from Wikidata or Turkish Wikipedia; the 94 without a single TA concept are territories, brainstem levels, composite meshes and umbrella pathways, for which the closest FIPAT component terms are listed and whose names will be authored.

`apply` takes every row whose relation is exact or synonym and is not flagged ambiguous (a synonym match must be confirmed by at least two sources), plus every row with a reviewer decision, and writes into the entry's JSON: `latin`, the FIPAT term with TNA first (traditional word order, "Arteria cerebri anterior") and TA2 as the fallback; `names.tr`, the Turkish display name, which is that Latin term because Turkish medical teaching names structures in Latin; and `synonymsByLang.tr`, Turkish search synonyms from the Wikidata label and aliases and the Turkish Wikipedia title and redirects. An existing `latin` is never overwritten without a decision: a value that is only a variant of the FIPAT term (word order, ae/oe spelling, a bracketed eponym, an extra qualifier) is kept as it is, and a substantively different one is kept and listed in the log for the reviewer. Entries without a FIPAT concept keep their English name in every locale. The four sources are registered in `pipeline/config/sources.yaml` (group `terms`) and have their own TERMINOLOGY section in `NOTICE`.
